The short answer

Face mode and edge mode differ in what a click picks, not in what gets cut. In face mode (2) you pick a flat panel of the brush; in edge mode (4) you pick one of the lines where two panels meet.

An edge is already a cut: it's the seam between two faces. So edge mode never cuts along the edge you picked. Its buttons use that edge as a pointer:

  • SPLIT EDGE adds a corner in the middle of that line. No new line appears; the two faces beside it just gain a corner each.
  • LOOP CUT cuts across the edge: it slices the faces on both sides of it and keeps going around the shape. The new line crosses your edge at its middle.
  • SELECT LOOP (or double-click) runs along the edge: it selects every corner on the line that continues straight on from it.

Every cut in the brush editor cuts faces. Face mode says "cut this face"; edge mode says "cut the faces this edge runs between". On a cylinder, pick a vertical side edge and LOOP CUT gives a horizontal ring, because the ring has to cross that edge.

The three parts of a brush

Every brush is made of the same three things, and each select mode picks one of them.

Faces are the panels you see and texture. Edges are the seams between them; each edge belongs to exactly two faces. Corners are where edges meet. A 16-sided cylinder has 32 corners, 48 edges and 18 faces (16 sides plus the two round caps).

The four select modes

The number keys 1 to 4 choose what a click on the selected brush picks; the gizmo and the panel buttons then act on that pick. In Edit Brush, a bar at the top left (Object · Face · Vertex · Edge) switches mode in one click. Its background is dark by default: change it (a color, the procedural sky or any skybox) on the brush's Brush View screen in the right panel.

In face, vertex and edge mode the whole-object move gizmo is hidden, so the brush stays highlighted and its edges are easy to click; 1 brings it back. Picking a face, edge or corner opens the Geometry screen of the right panel, where its buttons are.

Box select: grab a whole end at once

Box select has no button: it's a drag with the mouse in vertex mode. Use it to move a whole end of a brush, curves and all, e.g. to make a plate with rounded ends longer.

  1. Select the brush and press 3 (vertex mode).
  2. Press the mouse on empty space or on the brush (not on a corner dot) and drag: a dashed blue box follows the mouse. Let go and every corner inside the box is selected, including the ones you can't see at the back and underneath.
  3. Drag the gizmo (or press T, R or S to move, rotate or scale them). The selected corners move together, so the end keeps its shape and only the faces between the two ends stretch.
  • Shift+drag adds the corners in the box to the ones already selected; Alt+drag removes them.
  • A short drag (under about 5 pixels) counts as a plain click. Esc while dragging cancels the box.
  • Orbit the camera so the end you want is clear of the other end before you drag: the box takes everything inside it on screen, front and back.

SNAP sets the step for every face, edge and corner drag and for PUSH: off, 0.05, 0.1, 0.25 (the default), 0.5 or 1 m. It's in the DRAG group of the face card, and at the top of the Geometry screen in vertex and edge mode; one setting for all brushes, remembered. Corner sets keep their own 15° rotate and 0.1 scale steps.

In face mode the selected face's card is grouped: LOOK (material, TILE), CUT (SPLIT, INSET, LOOP CUT, FLIP FOLD), SHAPE (EXTRUDE, RECESS, DIST, OUTER WALLS), SELECT (FACE LOOP) and DRAG (MOVE / PUSH, SNAP, SOFT).

In every mode, Alt while dragging turns snapping off, Esc cancels a drag, and Cmd+Z undoes a whole drag or button press in one step.

What edge mode cuts, if not faces

It does cut faces. The edge you pick decides which faces and in which direction, and each button reads it differently.

  • Across vs along. LOOP CUT treats your edge as a rail it has to cross, so its new line runs at right angles to the edge. SELECT LOOP treats your edge as the first step of a line and follows it. Same edge, perpendicular results.
  • Why a vertical edge gives a horizontal ring. On a cylinder, the vertical side edges are the rails. A loop cut crosses each rail once at its middle, so the new vertices land at half height, all the way round.
  • Face mode gets there too. LOOP CUT H on a side face makes the same horizontal ring; the face and the H / V choice replace the edge as the pointer.
  • AROUND FACE n (one button per face the edge borders) selects the corners around the flat area on that side instead: on a plain cube's top-front edge, the top's outline or the front's. A flat area is every connected face in the same plane, so a top split into pieces still gives the whole top's outline. Hovering the button lights up that face.
  • SPLIT EDGE is the only one that works on the edge itself. It adds a corner to the existing seam. The faces on both sides gain that corner but no new edge crosses them, so they become five-sided.

Hovering SPLIT H / V shows a dashed line where the face will be cut; hovering LOOP CUT shows the whole ring, with a red dot where it would stop.

SPLIT also works on a face whose corners a ROUND turned into curves: it finds the face's four straight sides and cuts between the middles of two opposite ones, and each half keeps its share of the curves. INSET works on any face its border fits, at any curve size: where the border is wider than a curve, the inner face simply gets a sharp corner there. LOOP CUT still needs four real corners.

SPLIT and LOOP CUT need a face with four real corners. A point sitting straight along a side (left there by a neighbour's split) doesn't count as a corner.

Removing edges (DISSOLVE)

An edge can't simply be deleted: a brush is a closed solid, so taking an edge away would open a hole. DISSOLVE removes it the other way, by merging the two faces on either side into one.

  • DISSOLVE (edge mode, or Delete) removes the picked edge, or every edge picked with Shift+click. Corners left sitting in the middle of a straight line go too, so dissolving a SPLIT's line leaves a clean four-cornered face.
  • DISSOLVE LOOP removes every edge along the picked edge's loop (the line SELECT LOOP follows), e.g. a whole LOOP CUT ring.
  • Only edges between faces that are flat to each other can go. A corner edge, where two faces meet at an angle, is refused with a note: the merged face would have to bend. Each DISSOLVE is one Cmd+Z.

Walkthrough: a bulge around a cylinder

Five steps turn a plain cylinder brush into a bulged one; the screenshots are from the editor on a 16-sided cylinder.

  1. Select the cylinder, press 2 (Face mode) and click one of its side faces.
  2. Hover LOOP H (LOOP CUT H) in the CUT group: a dashed ring shows where the cut will go. Click it: the ring becomes real edges, and the panel says "Cut 16 faces, ring closed."
  3. Click SELECT RING next to that line. The editor switches to vertex mode with the 16 new corners selected, drawn in cyan and joined by a cyan line.
  4. Press S (or the SCALE button) and drag the gizmo's center box: right or up grows the ring, left or down shrinks it. The colored end boxes scale along one axis only.
  5. T moves the ring (up and down gives a higher or lower bulge), R tilts it. Each drag is one Cmd+Z.

A ring stops early, and the note says where, when it reaches:

  • a face that isn't four-sided (a cylinder's round cap, a triangle);
  • a face whose side doesn't line up with the ring, because it was split differently;
  • a point where an earlier split already meets the ring's path.

That's why LOOP CUT V on a cylinder side cuts only that one column: it runs up the side and stops at both caps. It's also why, after a SPLIT, a loop cut running the same way as the split stops after one face: the split left an extra corner in the middle of the neighbouring faces' sides. A loop cut across the split goes all the way round.

Several faces at once

Face mode can hold several faces, and the face buttons then act on them as one piece.

  • Selecting: Shift+click faces (in the view or the FACES list) to add or remove them; FACE LOOP H / V or a double-click selects the whole ring of faces through a face, e.g. every side of a cylinder.
  • Material and TILE in the "N FACES SELECTED" card apply to every selected face ("(mixed)" when they differ).
  • INSET puts one 0.25 m border round the outside of the selection, none between its faces: a 2 × 2 patch then RECESS makes one window; a cylinder band then RECESS makes one groove.
  • EXTRUDE / RECESS move the selection by DIST (0.25 m unless changed) as one piece: each face moves straight out (or in) along its own direction and grows (or shrinks) to stay joined, so a cylinder band becomes a thicker (or thinner) ring. Side walls appear only round the selection's outer edge.
  • A recess that would turn faces inside out is refused: the brush stays as it was and the card says why.
  • The gizmo moves every selected face together. SPLIT, LOOP CUT and FLIP FOLD work on one face at a time.
  • GIZMO: MOVE / PUSH. MOVE slides the selection one way with the arrows. PUSH swaps them for a green cube: drag it right or up to push every selected face out along its own direction, left or down to pull them in (in SNAP steps, Alt for free). The panel says how far it went, or where it stopped if going further would turn faces inside out.
  • EXTRUDE / RECESS DIST sets how far those two buttons go (0.25 m by default), for one face or many.

Outer walls: keep or follow

EXTRUDE, RECESS and PUSH can treat the sides around a face two ways; the OUTER WALLS row under DIST picks which, for one face or many.

  • KEEP (the default) adds walls all round the face. Where the face reaches the outside of the shape, those walls lie against the outer skin, leaving a paper-thin top or side over a pocket.
  • FOLLOW looks at the face on the other side of each edge. A face in the same flat side (the front's left half) stays and gets a step wall, as before. A face at an angle (a top, a bottom, an outer side) gets no wall: it's cut back (or stretched out) with the face. The top becomes an L, the right side gets shorter.
  • A face in the middle of a wall (a window after INSET) gives the same pocket either way.
  • Pushing so far that a side would turn inside out is refused with a note; nothing changes.

Soft falloff: smooth bulges and hills

Normally a drag moves only the corners you picked, so scaling one ring makes a sharp ridge and pulling one corner makes a spike. Turn on SOFT and the corners near them follow part of the way, less the farther they are: the same drags make rounded bulges, waists and hills.

  • Turning it on: the SOFT button at the top of the Geometry screen in face, vertex or edge mode, or O. It stays on for every brush until you turn it off.
  • RADIUS (meters) is how far it reaches. [ and ] shrink and grow it, also while dragging, and the result updates live.
  • SLOPE: Smooth (rounded, the usual choice), Linear (a cone or tent) or Sharp (a pointed peak with a soft skirt).
  • Distance is measured along the brush's surface, so the far side of a ring, or a part only reachable the long way round, stays put.
  • Green dots show which corners follow while you drag, brighter the more they follow. Each drag is still one Cmd+Z, and Esc cancels.
  • It works on every corner drag: a corner, an edge, a face or several, a corner set (move, rotate, scale) and PUSH. It needs corners to work with, so loop-cut a few rings first: on a plain 8-corner box it barely changes anything.

Rounding edges

ROUND turns a sharp edge into a curve of thin faces, like the corner of a phone or a worn stone block. The rest of the brush keeps its shape.

  • In edge mode (4) click an edge; Shift+click more to round several at once (the two top edges of a U, every upright edge of a box). They can share faces, but two edges that meet end to end at a corner can't be rounded together yet.
  • STEPS is how many thin faces make the curve: 1 is a flat cut-off, 3 a chunky low-poly round, 16 or more reads as smooth. SIZE is how far back from the edge the curve starts on each side (meters).
  • A SIZE that would run past the next corner is refused with a note; the brush stays as it was. The new curve takes the material of one of the two faces it joins; the ends (a cube's top and bottom) get a matching rounded corner.

Editing a curve later

  • A curve stays editable: right after ROUND it's open (lit green, with its card), and later you can click any of its faces or edges in face or edge mode, or press EDIT in CURVES ON THIS BRUSH (Geometry screen, every mode). STEPS and SIZE rebuild it, one Cmd+Z per visit; MAKE SHARP puts the sharp edge back; DONE closes the card. It's saved with the brush, so this works after Save and reopening the level too.
  • The ⋯ on a curve's row copies its STEPS and SIZE; on another curve's row, Paste applies them (one undo).
  • Edges rounded together are one curve and change together. SPLIT ALL makes each its own curve; SPLIT OFF on an edge's row separates just that one.
  • If you drag a curve's corners by hand it shows "edited by hand": STEPS and SIZE are locked (they'd undo your edits), but MAKE SHARP still works when it can. Curves rounded before this version have no memory and stay as they are.

Holes and pockets

HOLE cuts a round or square hole straight through a flat part of a brush, like a hole through a plate, or a pocket that stops partway with a floor.

  • In face mode (2) pick the flat face the hole goes into, then HOLE (CUT group). A see-through ghost of the cutter appears on the face and follows the mouse; click to drop it there (click again to move it). While the ghost is out, clicks place it instead of picking faces; Esc or CANCEL puts it away.
  • ROUND is a circle made of SIDES straight sides (24 by default; 6 for a hexagon) SIZE across; SQUARE takes a width and height. X / Y say where its center is, in meters from the middle of the face: type them for an exact spot.
  • DEPTH: THROUGH (on) goes straight in and out of the face on the far side. Turn it off and type a depth for a pocket with a floor.
  • CUT HOLE makes it. The ghost turns red, and the card says why, where a hole can't go: too close to an edge, coming out across an edge or through another part on the far side, deeper than the brush, or on a face that isn't flat. The hole's faces take the face's material.
  • A hole stays editable like a curve: it's open (walls lit green) right after the cut, and later you can click one of its faces or press EDIT in HOLES IN THIS BRUSH. Its settings rebuild it (one Cmd+Z per visit), PLACE brings the ghost back to move it (then MOVE HERE), FILL closes it and puts the face back.
  • Moving it with the gizmo: in face mode an open hole has its own move gizmo at its center, with arrows along the face. Drag it and the whole hole slides (in SNAP steps, Alt for free; one Cmd+Z per drag). Where it wouldn't fit, it stops at the last spot that does. To move just one of the faces around the hole instead, press DONE first, then pick the face. A hole whose corners you moved by hand can only be filled.
  • The rim is ordinary edges, so ROUND softens it; after that the hole counts as edited by hand.

Textures across edges

  • Textures wrap like gift-wrap: around the sides of a brush (sharp or rounded corners, any angle) and up over a rounded top edge, a texture runs on without a break. Seams stay only where the shape forces one: where the sides meet a flat top or bottom, and once where a full wrap comes back to its start. Faces only wrap into neighbors with the same material.
  • Each face is still lit on its own, so a curve made of many thin faces shows faint light bands; the texture itself runs on unbroken.

Bent faces and folds

A four-sided face whose corners stop lying flat has to crease along one of its two diagonals; the editor picks the one that bulges outward, and FLIP FOLD picks the other.

  • Why it creases. The screen draws every face as triangles, and a four-sided face is two triangles sharing a diagonal. While the face is flat that diagonal is invisible; once a corner moves off the plane, the face bends along it.
  • The automatic choice. Pull a corner out and the crease runs through it (the tent above). Push it in and the crease runs beside it instead, giving a diamond-shaped dent. Flat faces look exactly as before.
  • FLIP FOLD (Face mode, only on a bent four-sided face) switches that face to its other diagonal. The selected face shows its crease as a white dashed line, and the note under the buttons says "automatic" or "flipped". Flipping back to the automatic one clears the override.
  • The texture shows it more than the shading. Each face keeps one lighting direction, so the crease is easiest to see where the texture's grid lines bend.
  • Faces with five or more corners (from an earlier SPLIT next door) keep the plain crease, and FLIP FOLD is greyed out on them.

The brush editor (Edit Brush)

Edit Brush (Geometry or Actions in the right panel, on a brush) opens that brush alone at the center of an empty view, like prefab edit.

  • Convert to Brush (on a plain shape, same places) bakes its corners into an editable brush; its size params stop applying.
  • Save and Close in the amber bar at the top: Save (or Cmd+S) keeps you editing; Close leaves, and asks first if there are unsaved changes. Everything saved lands on the brush as one undo step.
  • A bar at the top left (Object · Face · Vertex · Edge) switches select mode in one click; 1 to 4 still work.
  • Background: dark by default. The brush's Brush View screen (right panel) picks a plain color, the procedural sky or any skybox; it's one setting for every brush, not saved in the scene. There's no fog and no solid ground, just a 30 m grid of 1 m squares.
  • Looking from below: right-drag orbits all the way under the brush (the level's camera stops at the horizon).
  • The level's spawn point and its right-click "Move initial spawn here" stay out of the brush editor.
  • From the prefab editor: a brush inside a prefab opens the same way (select it, Edit Brush). The bar says which prefab it's in; Close goes back to the prefab editor with your saved changes as one undo step there (its earlier undo steps are kept). Save the prefab afterwards to update every placed copy.

Export for 3D printing

Export for 3D printing… (Actions, on a shape or a selection of shapes) saves the shape as a file a slicer can print, at a size you choose, and can open it straight in Bambu Studio.

  • Why the size needs choosing: the editor works in meters, printers in millimeters. Copied as plain numbers, a 2 m brush would arrive as a 2 mm speck; converted, as a 2000 mm giant. So you pick the printed size: LONGEST SIDE in mm (100 by default), or SCALE (1 m in the editor = N mm). The dialog shows the result in mm and warns above a Bambu bed (256 mm) or below 2 mm.
  • 3MF (Bambu) states millimeters inside the file, so there's no guessing; STL works in any slicer (no units; slicers read mm).
  • The file goes to your Downloads folder (a name already taken gets -2, -3 …). Save and open in Bambu Studio also opens it there; Show in Finder reveals it.
  • The model is turned so the editor's up is the printer's up, centered and set on the bed. Several shapes become one model, placed as in the level. Just the shape is exported, not colors or textures; brushes are closed solids, which is what slicers need.
